Brief introduction

Yutian Ke

Postdoctoral Scholar in Geology, California Institute of Technology

I am a biogeochemist studying how physical landscape processes regulate Earth's carbon cycle and habitability across modern to geologic timescales.

Research framework

A geomorphic map for tracing carbon through landscapes

The themes are placed where they operate in the river corridor: critical-zone sources, active-channel transport, floodplain storage, and reservoir regulation.

Future research agenda

Earth surface processes and the carbon cycle

My research asks how sediment production, transport, deposition, and chemical alteration regulate carbon persistence from uplands to floodplains, deltas, reservoirs, and the ocean.

01

Source-to-sink river carbon

Quantifying how climate, tectonics, fluvial processes, and human perturbations shape the sources, composition, transport, and fate of organic carbon in river systems.

Global drainage basins
02

Floodplains, deltas, and carbon storage

Linking river migration, overbank deposition, and delta routing to determine the burial environment and timescales of preservation, degradation, and reworking of organic carbon.

Geomorphic carbon sinks
03

Changing and engineered river corridors

Testing how artificial reservoirs, land-use change, warming permafrost, and rapid environmental shifts alter carbon storage, erosion, and downstream export.

Anthropogenic perturbation
04

Data-rich Earth surface carbon cycles

Combining field observations, high-precision geochemistry, remote sensing, environmental datasets, and AI to identify the processes controlling terrestrial carbon sinks.

Lab, field, and computation
