Brief introduction

Yutian Ke

Postdoctoral Scholar in Geology, California Institute of Technology

I am a geochemist and geomorphologist studying how physical landscape processes regulate Earth's carbon cycle and habitability across modern to geologic timescales.

Expertise

  • Organic carbon transport behavior and dynamics in river corridors
  • Organic carbon preservation, degradation, and residence time
  • Organic carbon fluxes and stock
  • Coupled mud and organic carbon cycle

Research topics

Rivers, sediment, and carbon

How physical Earth-surface processes change the composition, transport, storage, and persistence of organic carbon.

01

Arctic floodplains

Quantifying how river migration, overbank deposition, and permafrost dynamics control long-term carbon storage and degradation.

Yukon River Basin
02

Sediment and carbon transport

Understanding how flocculation, grain-size sorting, and channel hydraulics regulate organic carbon fluxes and residence times.

Rivers and deltas
03

Human-regulated rivers

Tracing how cascade mega-reservoirs and other interventions change carbon sequestration and downstream composition.

Artificial reservoirs
04

Critical-zone processes

Linking hydrology, mineral interactions, organic carbon dynamics, and landscape evolution to biogeochemical cycles.
