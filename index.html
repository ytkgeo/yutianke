Future research agenda

Earth surface processes and the carbon cycle

Three connected questions guide my work: how climate change and human activities reshape landscape–carbon coupling; how mud and carbon move through channels and deltas; and when floodplain and delta deposits preserve or release carbon.

Landscape change 01

Carbon cycling in changing landscapes

I study how climate change and human activities reshape the coupling between landscape evolution and carbon cycling. My work examines how changes in permafrost, river morphology, and anthropogenic regulation alter the physical and biogeochemical functioning of river–floodplain systems across timescales.

Ke et al. (2025) Douglas et al. (2024) Smith et al. (2024) Floodplain OC stock (in review)
Transport 02

Sediment–carbon transport and routing

I study how fine sediment and associated organic carbon move through rivers and deltas. Mud aggregation and flocculation alter settling and characteristic transport distances, potentially changing where carbon is deposited, reworked, and ultimately exported from source to sink.

Ke et al. (2024) Flocculated mud (in review) Open datasets
Storage 03

Carbon storage, reworking, and persistence

Floodplain and delta deposition can temporarily remove organic carbon from active transport, while channel migration and other geomorphic processes eventually remobilize stored sediment. I combine geomorphic residence times with carbon composition and reactivity to determine when this transient storage leads to preservation, oxidation, or downstream export.

Ke et al. (2026) Geyman et al. (2025)
