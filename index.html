Future research agenda

Earth surface processes and the carbon cycle

My research asks how sediment production, transport, deposition, and chemical alteration regulate carbon persistence from uplands to floodplains, deltas, reservoirs, and the ocean.

01

Storage, degradation, and turnover

River corridors are the interface between continental erosion and long-term burial. Floodplains act as reactors in which material delivered from hillslopes is stored, altered, and re-entrained, and residence time controls whether carbon survives.

Floodplain residence time
02

Flocculation and carbon exposure

Organic carbon travels with fine sediment, whose behaviour is fundamentally altered by flocculation. Aggregation changes settling velocity and deposition pathways, and floc interiors may limit oxidation during transport.

Particle-scale controls
03

Disturbance and carbon fate

Landslides, riverbank collapse, thaw slumping, lake drainage, and flood scour rapidly mobilize sediment and organic carbon. I study them as Earth surface processes that initiate a new trajectory of transport, transformation, and storage.

Event-scale response
