Biography

From river sediment to Earth-system carbon dynamics

My research centers on a deceptively simple question: what controls the persistence of organic carbon on land? I approach this question through the movement of sediment, because sediment production, transport, and deposition are the physical pathways by which landscapes store, expose, transform, and export carbon.

I combine fieldwork, geochemistry, fluvial geomorphology, remote sensing, and large environmental datasets to connect processes observed in individual river corridors to carbon cycling across broader spatial and temporal scales. My work spans systems including the Yangtze, Yellow, Pearl, Amur, and Yukon river basins, with a particular focus on how floodplains, deltas, reservoirs, and permafrost regions regulate organic carbon fate.

Looking forward, I am building a research program that pairs field campaigns and geospatial analysis with high-throughput chemical and physical measurements of natural solids and liquids. The goal is to provide quantitative constraints on how Earth's dynamic surface regulates carbon storage, carbon release, and planetary habitability under environmental change.

River carbon across gradients

I study how climate, tectonics, sediment supply, and human perturbations shape organic carbon provenance, composition, transport, and preservation in river systems.

Floodplains as carbon archives

I use river migration, overbank deposition, geomorphic mapping, and radiogenic constraints to estimate carbon residence time, turnover, and degradation over timescales beyond direct observation.

Cross-disciplinary synthesis

I aim to integrate geospatial, geochemical, hydrological, and biological datasets with computational tools, including AI, to identify the environmental controls on terrestrial carbon sinks.

Research vision

Future directions in Earth surface carbon science

My future work builds from river-basin observations toward scalable models of how terrestrial carbon responds to climate change, landscape evolution, and human modification.
