Biography

From river sediment grains to Earth-system carbon dynamics

My research centers on a deceptively simple question: what controls the persistence of organic carbon on land? I approach this question through the movement of sediment, because sediment production, transport, and deposition are the physical pathways by which landscapes store, expose, transform, and export carbon.

I combine geochemistry and fluvial geomorphology with the aid of fieldwork, lab analysis, remote sensing, and large environmental datasets to connect processes observed in individual river corridors to carbon cycling across broader spatial and temporal scales. My work spans systems including the Yangtze, Yellow, Pearl, Amur, and Yukon river basins, with a particular focus on how floodplains, deltas, reservoirs, and permafrost regions regulate organic carbon fate.

Looking forward, I am building a research program that pairs field campaigns and lab analysis with high-throughput chemical and physical measurements of natural solids and liquids. The goal is to provide quantitative constraints on how Earth's dynamic surface regulates carbon storage, carbon release, and planetary habitability under environmental change.

River carbon across gradients

I study how climate, tectonics, fluvial processes, and human perturbations shape organic carbon provenance, composition, transport, and preservation in river systems.

Floodplains as carbon archives

I look into river migration, overbank deposition, and radiogenic constraints to estimate carbon accumulation, storage, degradation, and residence time in terrestrial depositional environments beyond direct observation.

Cross-disciplinary synthesis

I aim to integrate geochemical, hydrological, geospatial, and biological datasets with computational tools, including AI, to identify the environmental controls on terrestrial carbon sinks.

Published dataset

MOREPOC: a global map of riverine particulate organic carbon

MOREPOC v1.1 is an open, georeferenced database of suspended particulate organic carbon in global rivers. The map highlights catchments I have worked in by default; the grouped river/location-level MOREPOC data layer can be turned on when visitors want to explore values.
