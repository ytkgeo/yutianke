Field atlas / Alaska

Reading river landscapes from above

Selected aerial views of the Koyukuk and Yukon river corridors. Each site is organized around landforms and the questions they raise about sediment and carbon.

01 / Huslia, Koyukuk River

A floodplain built by a moving bend

01 / Active channel

The river occupies the margin of this bend. Erosion can return floodplain sediment and associated carbon to transport, but a single image cannot establish an erosion rate.

02 / Scroll-bar ridges

Arcing ridges and swales mark successive depositional surfaces on the inner bend. Their spacing reveals a spatial pattern, not the age of any one surface.

03 / Floodplain water

Water-filled lows and side channels interrupt the vegetated floodplain. They show how varied the depositional landscape is across a single bend.

Field photograph, not a georeferenced map Open large image →

Curving ridges and low, water-filled areas are visible across this bend. The pattern makes lateral accretion legible at a glance, while the channel along its edge points to the possibility of later erosion and recycling.
