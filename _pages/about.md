I am **Yutian Ke**, a Postdoctoral Scholar in Geology in the Division of Geological and Planetary Sciences at the **California Institute of Technology**. My research connects field sedimentology, isotope geochemistry, radiocarbon analysis, particle-size and mineral-surface measurements, and geospatial tools to understand how landscapes move and preserve organic carbon.

My current work asks why some river and floodplain environments become long-lived carbon reservoirs while others efficiently remobilize and oxidize organic matter. I focus on Arctic floodplains and deltas, large Asian rivers, and critical-zone systems where sediment transport, flocculation, reservoir trapping, and river migration regulate carbon burial and degradation.

## Research themes {#research}

### Carbon preservation in Arctic floodplains
I investigate organic carbon stocks, degradation kinetics, and residence times in discontinuous permafrost floodplains of the Yukon River Basin. This work combines sediment cores, floodplain chronosequences, radiocarbon, RPO thermal structure, grain-size distributions, and field observations of river migration to quantify carbon persistence over centuries to millennia.

### River transport of particulate organic carbon
Rivers connect hillslopes, floodplains, deltas, and oceans. I study how suspended sediment, particle-size divergence, flocculation, and channel cross-section heterogeneity change organic carbon fluxes and composition during transport, especially in the Huanghe, Changjiang, Yukon, and other large river systems.

### Human regulation of carbon cycling
Dams, cascade reservoirs, and water-sediment management can strongly alter particulate organic carbon sequestration and downstream composition. My work on the Changjiang basin examines how mega-reservoirs change carbon storage and export in one of Earth's largest river systems.

### Critical-zone and deep-time perspectives
I connect modern critical-zone hydrology and biogeochemistry with broader questions of Earth habitability. This includes secondary carbonate precipitation, organic carbon-mineral associations, carbon preservation in ancient river deposits, and implications for interpreting terrestrial and planetary sedimentary archives.

## Current projects

* **Arctic floodplain carbon storage:** quantifying organic carbon degradation and accumulation in discontinuous permafrost floodplains along the Koyukuk and Yukon rivers.
* **Oxbows as carbon sinks:** assessing how abandoned meanders and floodplain lakes can concentrate and preserve terrestrial organic carbon.
* **Flocculated mud and carbon transport:** testing how mineral-organic flocs regulate organic carbon loading, transport mode, and preservation in Arctic rivers.
* **Delta carbon fate:** tracing how particle sorting and sediment divergence create heterogeneous organic carbon pathways across deltas.
* **Storm and reservoir perturbations:** evaluating how storm events and cascade mega-reservoirs shift the age, composition, and sequestration of particulate organic carbon.
